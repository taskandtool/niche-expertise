# LinkedIn organic posts

Recipes for the owner's profile and the company Page. The worked examples use Harlow Joinery, a made-up workshop in Bristol that makes fitted kitchens. Jo writes the profile posts; milestones, hiring and customer wins go on the Page. Numbers in the examples are made up. Take a real post's figures from `claims.md`.

## What performs

- Engagement rate by impressions (Socialinsider, 2025 to 2026): native document 7.0%, multi-image 6.45%, video 6.0%, image 5.3%, text 4.5%, poll 4.2%, link 3.25%.
- Documents give small accounts (1k to 5k followers) the most impressions: 525 against 300 for text (Socialinsider). Buffer: PDF carousels get "3x more engagement than videos, 6x vs text". Sprout (Apr 2025): carousel reach is falling and short video under 60 seconds is growing. Expect the document figures to change.
- LinkedIn Pages guidance: "Companies that post weekly see a 2x lift in engagement"; "Images typically result in a 2x higher comment rate"; "custom image collages (3-4 images in one post) perform especially well".
- Dwell time is a signal. A post people read for 30 to 60 seconds ranks above a one-liner. The 1,300 to 2,000 character sweet spot comes from a paid report: unverified.
- Replying in your own comments: up to 2.4x reach (Sprout). First-comment links have "no negative impact" (Buffer, Dec 2025). Link posts rank last.
- Hootsuite: "LinkedIn is not designed for virality". Name the audience, state the problem, take a position.

## Norms

- Cutoff: the first 100 to 150 characters show before "see more". The first line is the hook and must stand alone. Mara Ellison, a news editor: "What's the most interesting part of your post? Put it at the top. Hook the people, tell them the payoff, and then get them into it."
- Paragraphs of one to three sentences with a blank line between. A concrete first line: a number, a named situation, a decision. Specifics over adjectives. A question only when you want the answer.
- Hashtags: three to five (LinkedIn Pages guidance), at the end. Mentions: five or fewer.
- Links: first comment. Alt text on every image, one literal sentence.
- Profile versus Page: opinions, stories and lessons from Jo's profile; milestones, hiring and customer wins from the Page, with Jo resharing (Sprout, Jul 2026). Notify employees of Page posts weekly (LinkedIn).
- Cadence for one person: two Page posts a week, two or three profile posts a week. Answer every comment in the first hour, and reply under your own post to keep it in people's feeds.

## Recipes

### Personal story with a lesson
When: the profile, once a week. The strongest dwell-time format for an owner.
Structure: line 1 is the moment. Three to five short paragraphs. End on the one thing you changed. A real photo.
Copy: "We turned down our biggest job last spring.

A developer wanted six kitchens in eight weeks. We said yes, then looked at the workshop calendar and rang back to say no. That call cost us about £40,000.

Now the calendar is on the office wall, and Sam checks it before either of us quotes."
Visual: a photo from the workshop that day, not a stock image of a handshake.
Pitfalls: a moral with no story under it, a story with no decision in it, a photo of a laptop.

### Contrarian take
When: the profile, when you disagree with common advice and can say why from your own jobs.
Structure: line 1 the common advice. Why it fails for your customers. What you do instead. An invitation to disagree.
Copy: "Everyone says get three kitchen quotes.

Three showrooms will price three different kitchens, and none of them has measured your room. The real price comes after a survey.

We survey on the first visit and quote from the drawing. We don't change that price later.

If you fit kitchens and think three quotes protect the customer, tell me why. I might be wrong."
Visual: optional. A photo of a drawing or a survey in progress.
Pitfalls: a take with no customer behind it, a strawman, arguing with a named competitor.

### "How we did X" breakdown
When: the profile or the Page, when you have real numbers. Best as a document.
Structure: the numbers up front, from and to in N weeks. Numbered steps. What did not work. The template in the first comment.
Copy: "From 11 weeks to 8 weeks per kitchen, across 14 jobs since January.

1. The same joiner measures and fits. No handover, no second measure.
2. We cut the carcasses the week the drawing is signed, not the week before fit.
3. One ply supplier, two deliveries a week instead of ad hoc.

What did not work: batching two kitchens at once. Both slipped.

The checklist we use is in the first comment."
Visual: a photo of the wall calendar or the workshop, or the same content as a 6-page document.
Pitfalls: numbers you cannot back, steps with no failure listed, the link in the body.

### Document / PDF carousel
When: the Page, twice a month. The highest-engagement format for small accounts.
Structure: 5 to 15 pages, landscape or square, PDF under 100 MB (Hootsuite). Page 1 the title and the promise. One idea per page. Last page a recap and a follow call to action.
Copy: the post itself is two lines. "How to read a kitchen quote: seven pages of what we'd ask if it were our house. #fittedkitchens #bristol #kitchendesign"
Visual: one layout, large type, a photo on every other page, page numbers.
Pitfalls: text walls, a cover that looks like an ad, a PDF exported from slides with the notes still on.

### Poll
When: the Page, once a month at most, for one real decision.
Structure: one line of context, two to four options, results shared in a follow-up post.
Copy: "We're choosing the next worktop to stock. Which would you pick for a family kitchen? Oak / Ash / Quartz / Stainless. Results and what we chose next week."
Visual: none.
Pitfalls: polls with an obvious answer, polls about nothing you will act on, no follow-up.

### Hiring post
When: the Page, reshared by the owner. Whenever a role is real.
Structure: the role, what month one looks like, the pay band, how to apply, an ask for shares.
Copy: "Harlow Joinery is hiring a bench joiner in Bristol.

Month one: you build carcasses from our drawings with Sam, then fit your first kitchen with Jo in week four.

£32,000 to £38,000 depending on experience, four-day week, workshop in Bedminster.

Email jobs@harlowjoinery.example with photos of two things you have made. No CV needed for the first message.

If you know a joiner who wants a four-day week, please share this."
Visual: a photo of the bench where they would work, with the current team in it.
Pitfalls: no pay band, "fast-paced environment", a list of adjectives instead of the first month.

### Client win with permission
When: the Page, with the client's written OK. The proof pillar.
Structure: the problem, what you did, the outcome in the client's words. Tag them only if they agreed. Thank them.
Copy: "Ruth Ellery's kitchen in Redland went in on 14 August, the date on the June drawing.

The problem: a 1900s wall 12 mm out of true and an alcove every showroom wanted to board over.

What we did: built the run into the alcove and scribed the end panel in the workshop, not on site.

In Ruth's words: 'The drawing was the kitchen. Nothing changed on fit day.'

Thank you, Ruth, for letting us share this."
Visual: two to four photos of the finished kitchen as a collage.
Pitfalls: naming a client without written permission, paraphrasing their words, a stock kitchen photo.

## Never

- Broetry: every sentence on its own line, dramatic one-word lines, a cliff-hanger before "see more", a moral at the end. Sprout: LinkedIn down-ranks "low-value content that prioritizes engagement mechanics over original insight".
- "Agree?" and "Thoughts?" as the whole call to action.
- More than five mentions.
- Reposts of other people's carousels, watermarked or not, presented as your own.
- Stock photos of handshakes, laptops and skylines.
